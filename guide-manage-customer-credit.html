How to Manage Customer Credit and Unpaid Invoices

By Oluwatobi Ayodele · Updated October 2026

Many customers ask to pay later. Offering credit can win loyal buyers, but unpaid debts are one of the biggest causes of small business failure.

Decide whether to offer credit at all

Ask whether you can afford to wait. If one late payment would stop you buying stock, limit credit to trusted customers or small amounts.

Set clear terms

Agree the amount, due date and payment method before the sale. Put it in writing, even as a short message or signed note.

Take a deposit

For bigger orders, collect part of the money upfront. This protects your costs and shows the customer is serious.

Record every debt

Keep a list with the customer name, date, amount owed and due date. Update it as payments come in. Never rely on memory.

Follow up early and politely

Remind the customer a day or two before the due date, and again when payment is late. A friendly message works better than an argument.

Know when to stop

If a customer keeps paying late, pause further credit until the balance is cleared. Offer a part-payment plan if it helps them settle.

Check the numbers monthly

Add up total money owed to you. If it keeps growing, tighten your terms. Your profit is only real when the money is collected.
