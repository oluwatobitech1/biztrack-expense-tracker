What Is a Break-Even Point and How Do You Calculate It?

By Oluwatobi Ayodele · Updated October 2026

Your break-even point is the amount of sales you need before you start making profit. Below it you lose money, and above it you earn.

The simple formula

Break-even sales = fixed costs divided by the profit you make on each sale (price minus variable cost). Fixed costs are things like rent and wages that stay the same. Variable costs are things like ingredients and packaging that rise with each item sold.

An example

A bakery pays 120,000 a month in rent, wages and electricity. Each loaf sells for 1,000 and costs 600 to make, leaving 400 per loaf. Divide 120,000 by 400 to get 300 loaves. Selling fewer than 300 loaves a month means a loss, and every loaf after that adds 400 in profit.

Why it matters

  • It gives you a clear monthly sales target.
  • It shows whether a price is too low.
  • It helps you judge a new cost, such as hiring someone or renting a bigger shop.

How to lower it

You can lower your break-even point by cutting fixed costs, reducing the variable cost per item, or raising your price carefully.

Keep it updated

Whenever rent, wages or supplier prices change, recalculate. Your records of income and expenses give you the numbers you need.
