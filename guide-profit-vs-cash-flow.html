Profit vs Cash Flow: What Small Business Owners Need to Know

By Oluwatobi Ayodele · Updated October 2026

Profit and cash flow sound similar, yet they answer different questions. Confusing them is one of the most common reasons small businesses struggle.

What is profit?

Profit is income minus expenses over a period. If you sold goods worth 500,000 and spent 350,000 producing and delivering them, your profit is 150,000.

What is cash flow?

Cash flow is the timing of money actually moving in and out. If customers owe you 200,000 that has not been paid yet, your profit looks healthy but you may not have cash to buy new stock or pay rent.

A simple example

A tailor finishes ten outfits in March and sells them on credit. She buys fabric and pays helpers in March but is only paid in April. March shows a profit on paper, yet her cash is low. Her business is fine, but she needs a plan for the gap.

How to protect your cash flow

  • Ask for deposits on larger orders.
  • Set clear payment dates and follow up promptly.
  • Keep a small cash reserve for slow weeks.
  • Negotiate longer payment terms with suppliers.
  • Record income when it is received so you can see real cash.

Which should you watch?

Both. Profit tells you whether your prices and costs work. Cash flow tells you whether you can pay bills on time. Tracking income and expenses regularly shows both.
