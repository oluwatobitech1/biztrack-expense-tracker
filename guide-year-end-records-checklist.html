A Simple Year-End Records Checklist for Small Businesses

By Oluwatobi Ayodele · Updated October 2026

A good year-end routine takes a few hours but saves stress for the entire next year. Use this checklist as a starting point. Tax and reporting rules differ by country, so confirm details with a local professional.

  1. Record everything. Make sure all sales and expenses are entered, including small cash items.
  2. Match your records to bank or mobile money statements. Investigate differences.
  3. Collect receipts. Store paper receipts in labelled envelopes, or photograph them and save the images.
  4. List who owes you and whom you owe. Note the amounts and dates.
  5. Count your stock and equipment. Write down what you hold and its approximate value.
  6. Review totals by category. See what grew and what shrank compared with last year.
  7. Calculate profit. Total income minus total expenses for the year.
  8. Export a backup. Save a copy of your records in at least two places.
  9. Set goals. Choose two or three clear targets for the new year, such as raising profit by 10 percent or reducing waste.

How long to keep records

Many countries require business records to be kept for several years. Check the rule that applies to you and keep your files safely.

If you record transactions regularly during the year, most of this checklist will already be done.
