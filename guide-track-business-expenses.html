How to Track Business Expenses: A Simple Step-by-Step Guide

By Oluwatobi Ayodele · Updated October 2026

Many small businesses know how much money is in the bank but not where it went. Tracking expenses fixes that, and it does not need accounting training. Here is a simple method you can start today.

1. Separate business and personal money

Use one account, wallet or mobile money line for the business. Mixing it with personal spending makes every later step harder and hides your real profit.

2. Record every expense the same day

Write down the amount, date, what it was for and who you paid. Memory fades quickly, and small purchases such as transport, airtime or packaging add up to a large share of costs.

3. Use a short list of categories

Five to eight categories are enough: stock or materials, transport, rent, utilities, wages, marketing, equipment and other. Too many categories make you inconsistent.

4. Keep proof

Keep receipts, invoices or a photo of them. They help if a supplier disputes a payment, and if you ever need to show records to a bank or tax office.

5. Review weekly

Spend ten minutes each week adding up spending by category. Ask which costs rose, which brought no return, and which you can negotiate down.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting until month end to record everything.
  • Not recording cash purchases.
  • Treating money you take for yourself as a business expense.
  • Never exporting a backup of your records.

The best system is the one you will actually keep using, so start simple and stay consistent.
