How to Price Your Products So You Actually Make a Profit

By Oluwatobi Ayodele · Updated October 2026

Many owners set prices by copying competitors. That can work, but only if your costs are similar. Start from your own numbers.

Step 1: Find your cost per item

Add materials, packaging and direct labour for one unit. If a batch of 20 meals costs 30,000 in ingredients and packaging, each costs 1,500.

Step 2: Add a share of overheads

Rent, transport, electricity and marketing also need to be paid. Add up a typical month, divide by the units you sell, and include that amount per item.

Step 3: Add your profit margin

Decide how much you want to keep. If your total cost is 2,000 and you want a 25 percent margin on the price, divide the cost by 0.75 to get about 2,667.

Step 4: Compare with the market

If your price is far above competitors, look for cheaper suppliers or offer something extra. If it is below, you may be undercharging.

Step 5: Review often

Costs change. Check your numbers every month or whenever a supplier raises prices.

Warning signs

  • You are busy but have little money left.
  • You never include your own time.
  • You give frequent discounts without checking the margin.

Accurate records of what you spend make every one of these calculations easier.
