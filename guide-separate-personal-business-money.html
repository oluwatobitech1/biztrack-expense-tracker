Why You Should Separate Personal and Business Money

By Oluwatobi Ayodele · Updated October 2026

Most small business owners start by using one pocket, one wallet or one bank account for everything. It feels easy, but it quietly makes the business harder to understand and harder to grow.

The problem with mixing money

When a customer pays into the same account you use for groceries, you cannot tell whether the business is making money. A good month can look bad because of a family expense, and a bad month can look fine because of a gift. You end up guessing.

Step 1: Open a separate account or wallet

Use a dedicated bank account, mobile money line or even a separate savings box for the business. All sales go in, and all business costs come out of it. Never pay personal bills from it directly.

Step 2: Pay yourself a fixed amount

Decide how much you will take home each week or month, and transfer it to your personal account. Treat it like a salary. Start modestly, and increase it only when profit supports it.

Step 3: Record every transfer

Each time you move money out for yourself, note it as owner drawings, not as a business expense. This keeps your profit figure honest.

Step 4: Borrow properly

If the business borrows from you, or you take money from the business in an emergency, write it down with the date and amount, and repay it when you can. Small loans between the two are fine when they are recorded.

Benefits you will notice

  • You see your true profit.
  • Banks and suppliers take you more seriously when your records are clean.
  • Tax and record keeping become simpler.
  • You stop spending working capital by accident.

It takes discipline at first, but within a month or two most owners say they finally understand their numbers.
